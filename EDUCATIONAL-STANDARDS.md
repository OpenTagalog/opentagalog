# Open Tagalog Educational Standards

**Version:** 1.0
**Project:** Open Tagalog
**Document Status:** Official
**Applies To:** All educational content published by the Open Tagalog Project

---

# Purpose

This document defines the educational standards of the Open Tagalog Project.

Its purpose is to ensure that every lesson, exercise, workbook, website page, audio recording, assessment, and future contribution follows the same educational philosophy and maintains a consistently high level of quality.

These standards apply to all contributors and should guide every educational and editorial decision.

---

# Mission

Open Tagalog aims to become the world's most comprehensive, professionally structured, and freely available Tagalog course for English speakers.

Every contribution should support this mission.

---

# Guiding Principle

> **When in doubt, optimize for the learner.**

This principle guides every educational and editorial decision within the Open Tagalog Project.

Whenever multiple valid approaches exist, contributors should choose the one that best supports learning while maintaining linguistic accuracy.

This principle applies to:

* Grammar explanations
* Vocabulary selection
* Lesson sequencing
* Exercise design
* Cultural notes
* Website navigation
* Visual design
* Accessibility
* Educational technology
* Future project development

Being technically correct is important, but helping learners understand is the primary objective.

Open Tagalog strives to remove unnecessary barriers to learning by presenting information in the clearest, most intuitive, and most approachable way possible.

Every contribution should ask one simple question:

> **Does this make learning easier?**

If the answer is yes, it is likely the right direction for the project.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. Learners Come First

Every educational decision should prioritize the learner.

If there is a choice between technical completeness and learner comprehension, choose the explanation that helps the learner understand while remaining accurate.

---

## 2. Explain Why

Do not simply present rules.

Whenever possible, explain **why** the language works the way it does.

Learners should understand the logic behind grammar instead of relying solely on memorization.

---

## 3. Teach Real Tagalog

Teach language that people genuinely use.

Avoid unnatural textbook examples unless they are specifically required to demonstrate a grammatical concept.

Whenever possible, examples should reflect everyday conversations in the Philippines.

---

## 4. Progressive Learning

Every lesson should build upon previous lessons.

New concepts should never assume knowledge that has not yet been introduced.

Difficulty should increase gradually throughout the curriculum.

---

## 5. Consistency

All lessons should follow the same structure and terminology.

Grammar terms, formatting, examples, exercise numbering, and lesson organization should remain consistent throughout the entire project.

---

## 6. Accuracy

Educational content should be linguistically accurate.

Whenever uncertainty exists, the issue should be researched and discussed before publication.

Accuracy is always more important than publishing quickly.

---

## 7. Openness

Open Tagalog is an Open Educational Resource.

Contributors should write content that is easy to share, improve, translate, and maintain.

Knowledge grows through collaboration.

---

# Educational Philosophy

Open Tagalog emphasizes understanding over memorization.

Students should be encouraged to discover patterns, recognize structures, and develop intuition for the language.

Lessons should promote active learning through reading, listening, speaking, and writing.

The objective is not merely to teach Tagalog, but to help learners become confident, independent users of the language.

---

# Language Standards

## English

All explanations should use clear, natural English.

Avoid unnecessary technical terminology whenever simpler language communicates the same idea.

Define technical linguistic terms before using them.

---

## Tagalog

Tagalog examples should sound natural.

Avoid invented sentences that native speakers would rarely say.

Whenever appropriate, indicate whether a word, phrase, or expression is:

* Formal
* Neutral
* Casual
* Colloquial
* Regional
* Literary
* Archaic

Whenever multiple common forms exist, explain both instead of implying that only one is correct.

---

# Vocabulary Standards

Vocabulary should be selected according to practical usefulness.

Priority should be given to language learners are most likely to encounter in everyday life.

Early lessons should emphasize:

* Greetings
* Family
* Food
* Transportation
* Shopping
* Directions
* Time
* Numbers
* Daily activities
* Work
* Health
* Common conversations

Less common vocabulary should only be introduced after learners have mastered the fundamentals.

---

# Grammar Standards

Grammar should be introduced gradually.

Whenever practical, each lesson should introduce only one major grammar concept.

Complex topics should be divided across multiple lessons.

Grammar explanations should always include:

* Clear explanations
* Step-by-step reasoning
* Natural examples
* Common mistakes
* Practice exercises

---

# Example Standards

Examples should:

* Sound natural.
* Reflect real conversations.
* Demonstrate one concept at a time.
* Use previously learned vocabulary whenever practical.
* Increase gradually in complexity.

Whenever practical, examples should reflect Filipino culture and everyday situations.

---

# Exercise Standards

Exercises should reinforce understanding rather than encourage memorization.

A balanced lesson should include a variety of exercise types, including:

* Reading
* Translation
* Fill-in-the-blank
* Sentence construction
* Error correction
* Matching
* Listening
* Speaking
* Writing

Exercises should gradually increase in difficulty.

Review exercises should regularly revisit previously learned material.

---

# Answer Keys

Every exercise should include an answer key.

Whenever possible, answer keys should explain **why** an answer is correct rather than simply presenting the correct solution.

Alternative correct answers should be acknowledged whenever appropriate.

---

# Cultural Notes

Language and culture are closely connected.

Lessons should regularly include cultural notes explaining customs, etiquette, traditions, history, and common expressions.

Cultural notes should inform rather than stereotype.

---

# Accessibility

Educational materials should be usable by as many learners as possible.

Content should:

* Work well on desktop and mobile devices.
* Print cleanly in black and white.
* Use accessible typography.
* Maintain high visual contrast.
* Avoid unnecessary visual clutter.
* Follow accessible web design principles whenever practical.

---

# Inclusivity

Examples should represent a wide range of everyday situations.

Content should welcome learners from every country, culture, and background.

The project should foster curiosity, respect, and appreciation for the Filipino language and its speakers.

---

# Open Educational Resource Principles

Open Tagalog is committed to the principles of Open Educational Resources.

Educational content should be:

* Freely available.
* Openly licensed.
* Easy to improve.
* Easy to translate.
* Easy to redistribute.
* Easy to preserve for future generations.

Contributors are encouraged to improve existing content whenever they identify opportunities to enhance clarity, accuracy, or educational value.

---

# Review Process

Before publication, educational content should be reviewed for:

* Linguistic accuracy
* Educational quality
* Clarity
* Consistency
* Formatting
* Grammar and spelling
* Accessibility
* Completeness

Major curriculum changes should be discussed before implementation.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Open Tagalog is intended to become a permanent educational resource that remains freely available for future generations.

Contributors are encouraged to make decisions that prioritize long-term quality, maintainability, and educational value over short-term convenience.

---

# Guiding Question

Before publishing any educational content, ask:

> **Will this genuinely help someone learn Tagalog better?**

If the answer is no, improve it before publishing.

---

# Our Promise

Open Tagalog is built on the belief that high-quality education should be freely available to everyone.

Every lesson, every explanation, every exercise, and every improvement should move us closer to a single goal:

> **To make learning Tagalog clear, enjoyable, accessible, and free for everyone, everywhere.**
